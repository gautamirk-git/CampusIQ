# Georgia Tech Coulter BME Undergraduate Program

## Program Overview

The Wallace H. Coulter Department of Biomedical Engineering offers a Bachelor of Science in Biomedical Engineering that is jointly offered by Georgia Tech and Emory University. The program combines "engineering fundamentals with hands-on design, problem-solving, and clinical relevance."

The Bachelor of Science in Biomedical Engineering degree program at Georgia Tech is accredited by the Engineering Accreditation Commission of ABET under Bioengineering and Biomedical Engineering program criteria.

## Curriculum Features

The program emphasizes several key approaches:

- Problem-Based Learning (PBL): "Real clinical problems drive classroom instruction and lab work."
- Integrated Design Spine: A four-year sequence of design courses teaching prototyping, testing, and solution communication
- Engineering Fundamentals: Mathematics, physics, mechanics, electronics, and computing
- Biological and Clinical Foundations: Anatomy, physiology, and molecular biology
- Electives and Concentrations: Options in robotics, data science, imaging, or pre-health preparation
- Capstone Design: Senior project course that brings student teams together with industry or clinical mentors

## Available Minors

Students can pursue complementary minors including:
- Minor in Biomedical Engineering
- Minor in Applications of Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning

## Capstone Experience

Senior students work in interdisciplinary teams to identify unmet clinical needs and develop innovative biomedical solutions. Projects are showcased at the Georgia Tech Capstone Design Expo, described as "one of the largest student design showcases in the country."

## Beyond Classroom Opportunities

- Undergraduate research opportunities
- Study abroad programs in Ireland and Japan
- Student organizations including BMES and Medical Robotics Club
- Entrepreneurship through Create-X and InVenture Prize programs

## Department

The Department is a partnership between the Georgia Tech College of Engineering and the Emory University School of Medicine dedicated to improving health and well-being by creating medical breakthroughs driven by engineering innovation and translational research.
