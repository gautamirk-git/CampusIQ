=== ABOUT RIVERBEND UNIVERSITY ===

Riverbend University is a mid-sized private university founded in 1962, located in
Millfield, Oregon. It enrolls approximately 8,200 undergraduate students and 1,500
graduate students. The university is organized into five colleges: the College of
Arts & Sciences, the College of Engineering, the School of Business, the School of
Education, and the School of Nursing.

The academic year is divided into two 15-week semesters (Fall and Spring) plus an
optional 8-week Summer session. Riverbend's mascot is the River Otter, and its
athletic teams compete in NCAA Division II as the "Riverbend Otters."

=== ADMISSIONS ===

Riverbend uses a holistic admissions process. Applicants submit the Common Application
or Riverbend's own application portal, along with:
- Official high school transcript
- One letter of recommendation
- A 500-word personal essay
- Optional: SAT or ACT scores (test-optional policy in effect since 2021)

Application deadlines:
- Early Action: November 1 (non-binding, decisions released by December 15)
- Regular Decision: January 15 (decisions released by March 15)
- Transfer students: rolling admission, priority deadline April 1 for Fall entry

The regular decision acceptance rate for the most recent entering class was 68%.
The middle 50% unweighted high school GPA for admitted students was 3.4–3.9.

International students must also submit TOEFL (minimum score 80) or IELTS (minimum
score 6.5) unless they attended a secondary school where English was the language
of instruction for at least three years.

=== COST OF ATTENDANCE (CURRENT ACADEMIC YEAR) ===

Estimated annual costs for a full-time undergraduate student living on campus:

- Tuition: $42,500
- Mandatory fees: $1,850
- Room and board (standard double, 14-meal plan): $13,200
- Books and supplies (estimated): $1,200
- Personal expenses (estimated): $2,000

Total estimated cost of attendance: approximately $60,750 per year.

Riverbend guarantees to meet 100% of demonstrated financial need for admitted
first-year students, as determined by the FAFSA and CSS Profile. About 72% of
undergraduates receive some form of need-based or merit-based aid. The average
need-based grant (excluding loans) for the most recent first-year class was
$28,300.

Merit scholarships (no separate application required; awarded automatically at
admission based on the application):
- Trustee Scholarship: $24,000/year, requires 3.8+ GPA and top 10% class rank
- Dean's Scholarship: $16,000/year, requires 3.5+ GPA
- Founder's Scholarship: $9,000/year, requires 3.2+ GPA

Payment plans are available through the Student Accounts Office, allowing tuition
to be split into 4 or 10 monthly installments per semester.

=== COURSES AND ACADEMIC PROGRAMS ===

Riverbend offers 58 undergraduate majors and 22 graduate programs. Popular
undergraduate majors include:

College of Engineering:
- B.S. Computer Science (also offers a Data Science concentration)
- B.S. Mechanical Engineering
- B.S. Civil Engineering
- B.S. Electrical & Computer Engineering

College of Arts & Sciences:
- B.A. Psychology
- B.A. English
- B.S. Biology (Pre-Med track available)
- B.A. Economics
- B.A. Environmental Studies

School of Business:
- B.S. Business Administration (concentrations in Finance, Marketing, and
  Entrepreneurship)
- B.S. Accounting

School of Nursing:
- B.S. Nursing (BSN) — accepts a limited cohort of 90 students per year; requires
  a separate secondary application after admission to the university, submitted
  by February 1 of the applicant's first year.

Graduate programs of note:
- M.S. Computer Science (thesis and non-thesis tracks)
- M.B.A. (full-time 2-year and part-time evening formats)
- M.S. Data Analytics
- Master of Education (M.Ed.)

Standard undergraduate degrees require 120 credit hours; the B.S. Nursing requires
128 credit hours due to clinical requirements. Most courses are 3 or 4 credit
hours. Class sizes for introductory courses average 35 students; upper-division
seminars average 15 students. The student-to-faculty ratio university-wide is 13:1.

Core Curriculum: all undergraduates, regardless of major, must complete Riverbend's
"Foundations" core curriculum, which includes:
- 2 courses in Written Communication
- 2 courses in Quantitative Reasoning
- 1 course in Global Perspectives
- 1 course in Ethics & Values
- 2 courses in Natural Sciences (at least one with a lab)
- 1 capstone course in the student's major, taken senior year

=== ACADEMIC CALENDAR HIGHLIGHTS ===

- Fall semester move-in: last Saturday of August
- Fall semester classes begin: first Tuesday after Labor Day
- Fall semester final exams: mid-December (exact dates vary by year)
- Winter break: mid-December through mid-January
- Spring semester classes begin: mid-January
- Spring semester final exams: early May
- Commencement: second Saturday in May

=== STUDENT LIFE ===

Riverbend has over 120 registered student organizations, including a student
newspaper (The Riverbend Current), a debate team, and a chapter of Model United
Nations. About 18% of students participate in one of Riverbend's 12 fraternities
and sororities. On-campus housing is guaranteed for first- and second-year
students; approximately 55% of all undergraduates live on campus.
