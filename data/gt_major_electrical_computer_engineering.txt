# Georgia Tech ECE Program Overview - Undergraduate Section

## Core Mission
The School of Electrical and Computer Engineering at Georgia Tech provides "comprehensive educational and research programs for undergraduate and graduate students" led by faculty recognized as leaders in their fields.

## Undergraduate Degree Programs
ECE offers three undergraduate pathways:
- Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering
- Bachelor of Science in Computer Engineering
- Joint B.S./M.S. Degree Program

Students can customize their studies through electives aligned with specific technical interests.

## Academic Opportunities
Beyond core coursework, undergraduates can participate in:
- International Plan
- Cooperative Education Program
- Research Option
(Completion of these programs results in special degree designations on diplomas)

Study abroad options include over 80 different programs, with one in France offering core ECE classes taught by Georgia Tech faculty.

## Research Involvement
Undergraduates access various research experiences through "short- or long-term individual or group projects" with hands-on involvement available early in studies.

## Career Outcomes
The program highlights that graduates work across industries including hybrid vehicles, smart homes, clean energy, and biomedical innovation. Starting salaries for bachelor's degree holders average over $65,000/year.

All ECE undergraduate and graduate programs are in the top 10 of the most recent college rankings by U.S. News & World Report. Graduates of ECE at Georgia Tech are among the highest paid of all engineering disciplines.

As of Fall 2023, the school had 143 staff members and 2,543 total students, making it one of the largest Schools of Electrical and Computer engineering in the world.

## Capstone Experience
The yearlong senior design project enables students to "develop life-changing technologies across myriad disciplines."
