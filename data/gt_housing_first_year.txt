# New to Georgia Tech Housing Guide

## Page Overview

This is Georgia Tech's comprehensive guide for first-time residents navigating the housing application and move-in process.

## Housing Application Process Checklist

1. Get admitted to GT and pay admissions deposit
2. Log into My Housing and complete application
3. Pay one-time $80 housing application fee and sign contract
4. Consider applying to Living Learning Communities
5. Find your roommate
6. Select your room or be assigned
7. Make move-in appointment
8. Complete self check-in process

## Key Information

Application Fee: One-time $80 (non-refundable for first-time applicants)

Contract Duration: Standard contracts cover full academic year (fall and spring); summer and spring-only options available

Meal Plans: Required for new first-year students; separate from housing costs; selected within housing application

Roommate Selection: Options include random assignment, matching with friends, or filling preferences

Move-In Dates: August 15-16, 2026 for new first-year, transfer, and graduate students

## What's Included in On-Campus Housing

Essentials & Services:
- Fully furnished rooms with all utilities
- Free WiFi across campus
- Free laundry facilities
- Dedicated technology support

Community Spaces:
- Communal kitchens (bring own cookware)
- Social and study lounges
- Shared floor bathrooms (traditional halls)

Residential Support:
- Dedicated tutoring for first-year courses
- Resident Assistant programming
- Quiet hours policies

Safety & Security:
- Georgia Tech Police partnership
- Electronic card access (BuzzCard instead of keys)
- RAs and Hall Directors on duty

## Living Learning Communities

Georgia Tech's First-Year and Transfer Living Learning Communities (LLCs) offer students a unique residential experience that blends academic interests with social engagement.

Communities feature specialized courses, dedicated study spaces, and faculty/industry expert interaction designed to ease college transition.

Living Learning Community Options:
- First-Year & Transfer Living Learning Communities
- Upperclass Living Learning Communities
- iHouse Living Learning Community
- Women, Science and Technology Living Learning Community
- John H. Martinson Honors Program

## Room Dimensions

Traditional Buildings:
- East Campus: approximately 12' X 10' to 15' X 11'
- West Campus: approximately 15' X 11'

Suite Style:
- Harris: 12' X 10'
- Woodruff: 15' X 12'

Apartments:
- Bedrooms: approximately 11' X 8' 10"
- Windows: 44" X 95" to 51" X 116"

Note: Lofts cannot be constructed in residence hall rooms.

## Communications from Housing

Email: Sent to official Georgia Tech email about contracts, alerts, newsletters (for recently accepted students, the application email is used initially).

Text Messages: Updates and facilities emergencies; only available for U.S.-based numbers.

## Important Policies

Insurance: Georgia Tech insurance does not cover students' personal belongings. Personal renter or homeowner's insurance recommended.

First-Year Residence Requirement: While most first-year students live on campus (97 percent of first-year students choose to live on campus each year), it is not required. First-year students who live on campus are required to purchase a meal plan.

Campus Address: After registering for classes, activate gtAccount at passport.gatech.edu. Campus mailing address and mailbox information available through Georgia Tech Post Office. Deliveries are NOT made to residence halls.

Virtual Tours: Georgia Tech does not offer tours of the residence halls at any time due to privacy reasons. 360° virtual tours available online for each building.

## Housing Types (First-Year)

Traditional halls belong to the First-Year Wellness Experience, in which two residents (sometimes four) share a bedroom and include either a suite-style bathroom that serves two bedrooms, or a community bathroom on a gendered floor. Apartments are available as overflow options only for first-year students.

## The First-Year Wellness Experience (FYWE)

The First-Year Wellness Experience is designed to support the personal, academic, and professional growth of first-year students by fostering a strong first-year cohort that centers well-being. This comprehensive program focuses on all eight dimensions of wellness: financial, spiritual, environmental, intellectual, emotional, social, physical, and occupational.

## Housing Fees

A $40 per semester fee is assessed to on-campus, first-year residents to support Georgia Tech's first-year programming and Week of Welcome initiatives.

## Off-Campus Housing

Alternative off-campus options available through Georgia Tech's Off-Campus Housing Marketplace for students choosing not to live on campus.
